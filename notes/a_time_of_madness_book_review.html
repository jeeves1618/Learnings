It is refreshing to read the perspective of a Pakistani liberal reflecting critically on his own country and its history. Salman Rashid tells the story of Partition with remarkable empathy, bringing to the fore the human tragedies that are often lost in the grand narratives of nation-building.

The most moving part of the book is the author’s journey across the border in search of his grandfather and aunts, who lost their lives during the traumatic Partition of India. His travels through Delhi, Sialkot and Jalandhar, nearly six decades later, in search of the remnants of his family’s past, are both poignant and haunting. These journeys are not merely an attempt to reconnect with his ancestors; they are also an effort to make sense of a tragedy whose consequences continue to shape the subcontinent.

Behind every border are people; behind every political decision are consequences that cannot always be undone.

What makes the narrative particularly powerful is its reminder that ordinary people paid the price for decisions made by political leaders who had little regard for the suffering those decisions would unleash. The violence surrounding Direct Action Day and the subsequent Partition exposed the terrible gap between the ambitions of those seeking political power and the fate of the millions caught in the upheaval. Families were torn apart, lives were extinguished, and generations were left with memories of homes and loved ones they could never recover.

A Time of Madness is a moving, thoughtful and deeply human book. It reminds us that behind every historical event are individual tragedies, and that the cost of political ambition is too often borne by people who had little say in the decisions that changed their lives. A compelling read for anyone interested in Partition, the history of the subcontinent, and the enduring human cost of political ambition.

The verdict

A compassionate, haunting journey into the personal aftermath of Partition—and a timeless warning about the distance between political ambition and ordinary people’s lives.
