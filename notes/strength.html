One Good Idea Stretched into an Entire Book

From Strength to Strength

From Strength to Strength has a single genuinely useful concept: the distinction between fluid and crystallized intelligence. It’s a helpful framework—but it could have been explained clearly in a thoughtful blog post or a five-page pamphlet.

One Useful Idea

The book's central distinction between fluid intelligence and crystallized intelligence is genuinely useful. The problem, in my view, is how much material is built around that relatively simple idea.

Instead, the book expands that core idea into chapters of recycled wisdom, subjective reflections, and familiar self-help themes that anyone who reads broadly or listens to decent podcasts has likely encountered many times before. Much of it feels like known insights repackaged and padded to fill pages.

The Opportunity Cost of Reading

My bigger frustration is the opportunity cost. In a world already overflowing with words—especially now, when Large Language Models can generate endless text—readers have to be careful about what earns their time.

This book did not earn mine.

Who Might Enjoy It?

If you’re completely new to personal development literature, you may find it helpful. But if you’ve read even a handful of thoughtful books in this space, there’s very little here that justifies the length—or the price.
